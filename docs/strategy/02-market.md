# B. Market and positioning

Researched 2026-10-18 from public pages. Prices and traffic change; third-party numbers are marked (3rd-party), and
claims not checked are marked **unverified**. Sources are at the end.

## What the comparison teaches

1. **Notes tools publish gardens, not catalogs.** Obsidian Publish, Heptabase and Notion publish one person's
   pages; none gives the reader progress, and Notion's catalog spreads through copies (templates).
2. **Catalogs of paths have progress but not notes.** roadmap.sh and Trailhead let a learner follow someone's
   path with their own progress; the nodes are topics with resource links, not the curator's linked notes.
3. **Public user content works only with curation.** Quizlet pays for moderation and takedowns; AnkiWeb's ratings
   are gamed; Notion's Marketplace is editorial (featured, collections). Google's spam policies (scaled content abuse,
   since March 2024, applies to human-made pages too) and its UGC guidance (`rel="ugc"`, `noindex` for new users
   until they earn trust) point the same way.
4. **roadmap.sh's traffic is mostly direct and brand** (Semrush and Similarweb, 3rd-party), built on a GitHub repo
   with ~370k stars and a developer community; its organic search grew through hub pages written by paid writers
   (a vendor case study). A one-person site cannot copy the flywheel, but can copy the shape: a hub (root page)
   with spokes (pages) linking back. That is already WisePath's structure.
5. **Free-forever products are funded by donations at scale** (Khan, Wikimedia, freeCodeCamp) or by being someone's
   marketing (Trailhead). Class Central is the one example of a catalog funded by affiliates, after about a decade
   of editorial content.

## Did anyone combine all four?

Personal linked notes, a map read from the links, a reader's own progress on someone else's path, and a public
catalog: none of the products above has all four. That is not proof of uniqueness: indie tools, app stores and
Product Hunt were not searched exhaustively, and several pages did not render for the researcher. Do not claim
"the only".

## Positioning for WisePath

**Recommended:** *"Study paths written by someone who studied it: their notes, linked as a map, with your own
progress. Free, and yours to write too."*

| Opportunity | Evidence | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| A path is the author's notes, not a list of topics | roadmap.sh and Trailhead nodes are topics and resources; WisePath pages are explanations with sources | Only valuable if the notes are good: quality of the first paths decides everything |
| Follow without copying | Notion, Quizlet, Anki spread by copies; WisePath's "take" keeps one living path with each learner's marks | A learner loses their marks if the author makes the path private (see decision G11) |
| Self-learning outside programming, in Portuguese | roadmap.sh is developer and India/US heavy; Brazilian sites target school and ENEM | School keywords are owned by giant pt-BR sites: aim at long-tail self-study topics, not ENEM |
| Free and no AI | Competitors' paid tiers are built around AI | A trust message, not a proven reason to choose |

Not recommended as positioning: "graph of knowledge" (Obsidian owns it), "better roadmap.sh" (developer niche is
taken and community-driven), "Notion for study" (copy model, different product).
